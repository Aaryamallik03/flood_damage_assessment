Rate flood damage to a building from two images

Upload a before and an after image of the same building. The model labels the damage as none, minor, major or destroyed. This is a research prototype. It was trained on xBD flood imagery and has not yet been tested on the August 2026 flood in Nepal.

The model was trained on square crops centred on one building. Use a crop of a single building at the same location in both images. Whole scenes give unreliable results. Images are processed in your browser and never uploaded; see the privacy policy.

The flood in Nepal

On the morning of 26 August 2026 a sudden surge of water came down the Bhote Koshi river from the Tibet border after a high-altitude rock and ice failure. It moved through Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading along the Trishuli corridor and destroyed homes, bridges, roads and hydropower facilities.

Nepal's disaster authority, NDRRMA, reported 675 deaths as of 29 August, with about 2,500 people still out of contact. IOM estimated that 84,270 people were affected across six districts. These figures were still changing when this page was written, so check NDRRMA for current numbers. Other sources: UNDP Nepal and Copernicus Emergency Management Service.

The model was trained on other flood events from the xBD dataset. This was a steep-valley flash flood carrying debris, which looks different from much of that data. Results on Nepal imagery test the model. They are not a verified damage assessment. For real decisions use Copernicus EMS maps and field assessments by NDRRMA and the Nepal Red Cross.

Pre- and post-event imagery is available through the International Charter on Space and Major Disasters (activation 1052) and Copernicus EMS. The next step is to crop individual buildings from it and compare the model's ratings with the published damage maps.

Method

The classifier is a Siamese network. One ResNet-50 encoder, shared by both images, turns each image into a feature vector.

  1. Crop a 224 by 224 patch around the building, before and after.
  2. Encode each patch with the shared ResNet-50.
  3. Join the two feature vectors (2048 plus 2048 values).
  4. Pass them through a small network (4096 to 512 to 128 to 4) that outputs the damage class.

A second component, still in development, maps flooded areas from SAR radar imagery, which works through cloud cover.

Results

The classifier was trained on 45,709 building samples from flood events in the xBD / xView2 dataset: 23,424 no damage, 9,360 minor, 10,302 major and 2,623 destroyed.

Limits

  • There is no independent, event-level test set yet.
  • Building crops must be supplied. Automatic building detection is not built.
  • The SAR flood segmentation has not been evaluated on held-out data.
  • The model has not been validated for any region, including Nepal. It is not a tool for rescue, insurance or official decisions. See the terms of use.
