Building Damage Assessment from Pre- and Post-Flood Imagery

A Siamese ResNet-50 model compares corresponding pre- and post-disaster building imagery and classifies damage as no damage, minor, major, or destroyed. This is a research prototype trained on xBD flood imagery and has not yet been independently validated on Nepal flood imagery.

01

Method

The building classifier uses a Siamese convolutional neural network with two branches that share the same ResNet-50 encoder weights. Each branch converts one image into a 2048-dimensional feature vector.

  1. Extract a building-centered patch from the pre-disaster image.
  2. Extract the same spatial region from the post-disaster image.
  3. Encode both patches using the shared ResNet-50.
  4. Concatenate the two feature vectors.
  5. Classify the resulting representation into four damage classes.
Second component: a SAR-based flood extent segmentation pipeline is also under development. It is not yet evaluated on a properly held-out dataset.

04

Dataset

The building damage component uses flood-related events from the xBD / xView2 disaster-damage dataset. Building-centered pre- and post-disaster image patches are extracted from corresponding spatial locations.

No damage 23,424
Minor damage 9,360
Major damage 10,302
Destroyed 2,623

Images are resized to 224 × 224 and normalized using ImageNet statistics. The class distribution is imbalanced, with destroyed buildings representing the smallest class.

05

The flood in Nepal

On the morning of 26 August 2026, a sudden surge of water came down the Bhote Koshi river from the Tibet border after a high-altitude rock and ice failure. It moved through Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading along the Trishuli corridor and destroyed homes, bridges, roads and hydropower facilities.

Nepal's disaster authority, NDRRMA, reported 675 deaths as of 29 August, with about 2,500 people still out of contact. IOM estimated that 84,270 people were affected across six districts. These figures were still changing when this page was written. See NDRRMA for current information.

Additional sources: UNDP Nepal and Copernicus EMS .

Why this matters for the model: The classifier was trained on other flood events in xBD. The August 2026 event was a steep-valley flash flood carrying debris, so its imagery and damage patterns may differ from the training distribution. Results on Nepal imagery should therefore be treated as an evaluation of generalization, not a verified damage assessment.

Pre- and post-event imagery is available through the International Charter on Space and Major Disasters and Copernicus EMS. A useful next step is to extract individual buildings from that imagery and compare model predictions against published damage maps and field assessments.

06

Limitations

  • No independent, event-level test set has been established yet.
  • The original training process used a random train/validation split, so related imagery can potentially appear across different splits.
  • Building-centered crops must be supplied; automatic building detection is not currently implemented.
  • The SAR flood segmentation component has not been evaluated on held-out data.
  • The model has not been validated for Nepal or another completely unseen geographic event.
  • The system should not be used as the sole basis for rescue, insurance, infrastructure, or other high-stakes decisions.
07

Next steps

  • Introduce reproducible event-level train, validation and test splits.
  • Evaluate generalization on completely unseen disaster events.
  • Validate performance on Nepal-specific flood imagery.
  • Add automatic building-footprint detection for inference.
  • Improve handling of the imbalanced damage classes.
  • Evaluate and improve the SAR flood segmentation component.
  • Add calibrated probabilities and additional error analysis.
  • Explore Grad-CAM or related explainability methods.
